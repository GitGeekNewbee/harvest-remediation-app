Fluency Gatekeeper

Adding and subtracting decimals requires strong carrying and borrowing skills. Pass 3 levels of whole-number arithmetic to unlock this module.

The 3-Level Pathway:
  • Level 1: Addition (Carrying over 10)
  • Level 2: Subtraction (Borrowing)
  • Level 3: Mixed Operations (Crossing 100)

Target: 15 questions per level at 90% accuracy.

Level 1: Addition

The Comma Anchor

If you add 3,4 and 1,25 like whole numbers without lining up the comma, you will add tenths to hundredths. This is entirely wrong.

The comma is your anchor. It must drop straight down.

By stacking the commas on top of each other, you guarantee that the Tenths line up with the Tenths.

The Placeholder Zero

Once the commas are lined up, you might have empty spaces hanging over the edge.

Fill the empty gaps so the numbers match in length.

Because 3,4 is the exact same as 3,40, we can drop a zero into the empty space. Now you can safely calculate straight down: 3,40 + 1,25 = 4,65.

The Subtraction Trap

Placeholder zeros are mandatory for subtraction.

Try calculating: 5 - 2,3

If you just drop the 3 down, you will get 3,3. This is WRONG.

Whole numbers have an invisible comma at the end. 5 becomes 5,0.

Now you have 5,0 - 2,3. You must borrow from the 5 to subtract the 3. The correct answer is 2,7.

Calculate the answer carefully:
=

Write it down on paper, line up your commas, and use placeholder zeros!
